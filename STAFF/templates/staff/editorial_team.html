How this newsroom works

The rules the publishing system actually enforces.

  • One format, one meaning

    Every story is written in plain paragraphs with the same small set of markers: # for a section heading, * for a bullet, 1. for a numbered point, **bold** and *italic* for emphasis, a pasted web address for a link, and imgl, imgr or imgc to place a picture. One marker does one job, so a line of ordinary text is never published as a heading by accident.

  • Headings are fixed once published

    A headline generates its web address the first time the story is saved and is never rewritten afterwards. Old shared and indexed links keep resolving to the same story, and a published title can never quietly change into something the original link does not describe. Correcting a headline means a deliberate delete and repost.

  • Two timestamps, never one

    Every story keeps the exact time it was published separately from the exact time it was last changed, so a correction is visible as a correction instead of being indistinguishable from the original.

  • Attribution on every story

    Each story carries its author, its category and its publication time. Every picture and file in the shared archive records who uploaded it and when, and is served from this site's own domain.
