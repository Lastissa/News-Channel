On the twenty-second of October, nineteen sixty-two, about three weeks after the nation's second independence anniversary, the first lectures were heard. Law: fifty-six students. Business and Social Studies: forty-six. The Medical School: twenty-eight. About one hundred and thirty in all; the university's own page says one hundred and thirty-one. And notice one more detail: the faculty began as Commerce and Business Administration, renamed Business and Social Studies at its Board's very first meeting. Two sites, one dawn: Surulere, and Idi-Araba, where tomorrow's doctors were already scrubbing in.
